About Me

I am a Computer Vision Engineer focused on applied AI and vision systems. My work spans the practical engineering lifecycle — from collecting and curating data to training, evaluating, deploying, and debugging models in real operating environments.

I build with OpenCV and deep learning frameworks (PyTorch/TensorFlow), and I have worked on detection, tracking, facial recognition, OCR, and vision-based analytics for operational use cases such as crowd/identity workflows, attendance, retail product recognition, and industrial inspection.

Real-world conditions matter here: occlusion, variable lighting, camera angle and field-of-view constraints, small objects, background complexity, perspective changes, and false-positive / false-negative trade-offs. A production CV system has to balance accuracy + latency + reliability + deployment constraints + maintainability — not just model accuracy.

From Model Accuracy to Production Reliability

A model that works on curated test data is only the start. Production CV systems must handle the realities of cameras, scenes, and compute that training data never fully captures.

Capture Constraints

  • Camera placement and angle of view
  • Field of view vs target size at distance
  • Lighting — day, night, shadows, glare
  • Motion blur on moving subjects
  • Perspective distortion and tilt

Scene Complexity

  • Occlusion by objects, people, structures
  • Background variation and clutter
  • Object size — small / distant targets
  • Similar appearances between classes

Model Behaviour

  • False positives — unneeded alerts or rejects
  • False negatives — missed detections
  • Model drift after deployment
  • Confidence & threshold tuning

System Constraints

  • Latency and real-time FPS requirements
  • GPU / compute and memory limits
  • Network bandwidth on video streams
  • Deployment environments — edge or cloud
